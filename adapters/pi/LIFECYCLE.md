# Shared lifecycle rules

These rules are shared by the nine manual Pi skills, in two scopes. **Contract reuse**, **Personal policy resolution** and **Reporting style** below apply to every skill, including `iwe-note-review` and `iwe-pack-use`: any skill that writes, archives, deletes, or reports to the owner resolves the owner's policy first. The remaining subsections — Weekly reconciliation cadence, Work-product context resolution and completion archival, and Planning-projection boundaries — govern the seven Session/Day/Week/Month lifecycle skills, which own planning surfaces; a non-lifecycle skill neither performs nor simulates those projections, and never writes a planning surface. Ritual-specific steps remain in the skills and reference these rules by name instead of restating them; upstream protocols remain the source of IWE concepts. Two conditional parts live next to this file: [WEEKLY.md](WEEKLY.md) (weekly steps of the Session and Day rituals, bounded WeekPlan exceptions), read when weekly files are in scope, and [ARCHIVAL.md](ARCHIVAL.md) (the completion-archival transaction), read only before proposing or performing an archival. Section names that skills cite resolve across the three files. Read this file before planning writes. Resolve it relative to the physical source of the selected skill, following only its reviewed deployment symlinks: `../../LIFECYCLE.md` from the skill directory. Preserve the template-relative source layout described in [README](README.md#manual-deployment). A missing or unreadable reference blocks the affected action; do not substitute an old memory copy or run deployment scripts.

## Autonomous preparation and consolidated approval

An explicit Session/Day/Week/Month ritual request authorizes its necessary in-scope read-only discovery, evidence reconstruction, and draft preparation. Do not ask whether to begin or continue those steps; report early/late timing without pausing or changing the period. This does not waive required configuration checks, repository instructions, diagnostic disclosure limits, or external-operation gates, and does not request another ritual.

Continue unaffected preparation when evidence is incomplete. Mark unchecked or conflicting facts as unknown; distinguish evidence, existing owner decisions, and unapproved recommendations. Propose reasonable choices for review rather than inventing acceptance, availability, priorities, or permissions. Collect material questions and suggested dispositions in the completed draft. Ask immediately only when an unresolved target, authority, or dependency prevents safe further preparation; block only the affected action. Missing optional input is not a reason to stop the whole ritual.

Before writes, present one consolidated proposal: required planning changes named by what they record, not by file (exact paths only in archival plans), material questions, and separately labeled optional captures and exact archival plans. One explicit reply may approve all listed actions or selected items; approval of the core summary alone does not approve optional actions. The owner's reply that answers the draft's questions or corrects it without raising a new decision approves the corrected draft: write at once and report what was saved, without restating the owner's words for another confirmation; an unanswered question stays open in the saved record and is not asked again in the same ritual. Reuse explicit approval for unchanged scope, including on retry only when durable intent required by the archival/reconciliation contract exists and expected prior state still matches. Ask again only for unresolved decisions, changed scope, conflicting newer state, or a new mutation boundary. Completion acceptance, weekly-intent changes, exact archival approval, and Git/publication/deployment/external-operation boundaries remain explicit; bundling does not waive them.

## Prepared drafts and short sittings

Personal policy may select prepared drafts for Week Close, Week Open and Month Close, so that they run in short sittings, possibly days apart, and any sitting can be the first in a fresh session:

- **Prepare.** Any call of the ritual (its `/skill:` command or phrase) with no draft does the read-only reconstruction once and writes it as a `## Черновик` section at the end of the ritual's target file: the paired WeekReport (Week Close), the next WeekPlan created with `status: draft` (Week Open), `archive/MonthClose YYYY-MM.md` created with `status: draft` (Month Close). The draft is the ritual's working memory, not a planning record: writing and updating it needs no approval and changes nothing else. It holds the preparation date, the evidence boundary (the latest DayPlan, Git period and ledger entries read), the proposed text and the open questions grouped by the ritual's parts. A plain call then shows the first part in the same sitting; a call that only asks to prepare ("подготовь …") ends there with one line on what waits for the owner.
- **Parts.** Each ritual names its parts; each fits one sitting and is finished on its own: its questions, its approval and its writes. An approved part is written at once and marked `✅ <date>` in the draft with the owner's answers; a later part does not reopen it unless the owner asks.
- **Resume.** When a draft exists, the ritual reads the draft instead of reconstructing, refreshes only evidence newer than its boundary (new DayPlan results, commits, ledger entries), says in one line what changed, then shows the first open part. One part per sitting unless the owner asks for more.
- **Finish.** After the last part, the draft section is removed; results stay where the parts wrote them. A draft whose period has already been followed by the next one is reported, not continued silently.

## Contract reuse

An explicit read of an applicable contract, including an expanded skill body, remains sufficient while its complete relevant content is available and no change, conflict, or freshness concern is evidenced. A new request or another WP registration does not by itself invalidate that read. After compaction, distinguish retained full text from summary-only knowledge: recover missing applicable contracts, not every file in the historical read list. A summary or read-file list is not a substitute for the contract. Stricter applicable instructions still govern.

Refresh mutable planning evidence separately: before writes, verify the selected WP identity/collisions and complete affected destination sections; verify current pair metadata only when weekly evidence or writes are in scope under Weekly reconciliation cadence below. Reuse other still-current context; do not reconstruct the entire week for a registration. Batch independent selected reads. Session and Day rituals read the WP registry selectively: its legend plus the rows for named WPs and open statuses, found with `rg` (for example `rg -n '^\| (WP-)?(12|7) |🔄|⏳|⏸' DS-strategy/docs/WP-REGISTRY.md`); Week rituals may read it whole. Keep required sections complete even when they exceed an ordinary output budget; use metadata/heading indexes to avoid unrelated sections, not line limits that silently omit requirements.

## Personal policy resolution

Before policy-dependent proposals or actions, explicitly read `DS-strategy/docs/PI-PERSONAL-POLICY.md` from the owning IWE workspace, reusing an available prior read under Contract reuse above. Resolve the workspace from the current session cwd and its ancestors: require one unambiguous root containing workspace `AGENTS.md` and the `DS-strategy/` governance directory, consistent with the applicable repository instructions. Do not resolve personal policy relative to this file or a symlinked skill's source; the template may live outside the workspace. Do not scan sibling workspaces, use recency, or assume a machine-specific root. Missing or ambiguous workspace/policy, unreadable policy, or conflicting instructions requires stopping the affected proposal/action and asking the owner; do not invent defaults or run recovery scripts.

The policy owns personal conventions and tool choices. Its conventions govern user-facing output as well as writes: apply the owner's naming, grouping, language, and reporting rules to every draft, plan, summary, and chat report, not only to files. The adapter owns its lifecycle approval and planning-write boundaries. File presence is not automatic Pi loading, and policy cannot expand execution authority. Ordinary WP continuity is defined by Session Open/Close, not by an external workflow.

One naming rule is stated here because it is silently skipped otherwise: **whenever a work product is mentioned, give its identifier together with its registered name**, resolved from the registry or its context — `WP-12 — Personal strategy for the year`. This holds in every ritual and every surface, including short lists, one-line mentions, status updates, tables, and chat answers; brevity is not an exception, and no ritual-specific step overrides it. When an action is needed, the action follows the name rather than replacing it: `WP-12 — Personal strategy for the year: review the draft`. An identifier alone, or an identifier followed by a description of the action instead of the name, is wrong. If the registered name cannot be resolved, say so explicitly instead of substituting a paraphrase. The owner's policy may extend this rule but does not restate it.

## Reporting style

One style for every ritual draft, final report, written planning result and chat answer. Skills say only what their draft contains and reference this section instead of restating it; the owner's policy may extend it.

- **Main result first.** Open with the main observable result and its human, product or functional value, then supporting work, then unfinished work and the next action. For a week or month, build it from the main-result sections of the period's DayPlans and reports, keeping the owner's own wording where it exists; never open with a checklist of commitments. A change in the way of working (a method, tool or system the owner built or adopted) and a hand-off to the next stage (merged, approved, released, accepted by others) are main-result candidates before the volume of output or engineering detail. When the owner has not yet named the main result, the draft asks for it and offers its own candidate. Technical detail only where it establishes a result or a material risk.
- **Value, not plan completion.** Planned and emergent work count at equal value. Plan completion (counts or fractions over the frozen baseline) is optional supporting information after the results, never the headline or the score of a period.
- **Visible weight.** Give the main outcome more space than secondary work; do not present active work products as equally important.
- **Names with identifiers.** The work-product naming rule above applies to every identifier shown to the owner, such as monthly results and decisions: identifier — name, then the assessment or action.
- **Only what matters.** Report state that affects a decision, creates a risk or contradicts the plan; successful checks and clean scans are not mentioned. Planning files get results and decisions, not the agent's verification log, temporary paths, tool runs or unrelated dirty repositories; verification goes briefly into the chat report.
- **Limits once.** State an evidence limit once, where it applies and only if it changes a decision, not as a disclaimer repeated after each achievement. Do not list what the agent will not do, does not claim or is not authorized to do, and do not comment on its own reasoning or evaluation method ("I do not infer…", "I will not judge by…"); approval boundaries are known and are not repeated in chat.
- **Progress as it is.** Describe work outside registered or planned work products as the progress it is; never qualify it by what it is not (not a work product, not complete, not accepted).
- **No restated method.** Planning files and chat hold today's facts and choices. Do not restate how a project is run, policy, invariants or what stays unchanged ("weekly commitments are not changed by this", "registry WIP is not human attention", "no numeric limit"); those live in their own sources. Standing project constraints (known mocks, provider targets) are named only when they change or affect today's decision, not repeated every day.
- **Unknowns.** Mention what is unknown or not yet said only when it blocks a decision, and then as a question, never as the owner's omission ("not reported", "not set", "not scheduled").
- **Achievement before status.** Each item opens with what was achieved, named by its result; a project's code, documents (ADRs), review preparation and communication form that project's one result, not separate items; a result beyond the planned item is reported as that result. A status word (`partial`, `unknown`) is shown only for unfinished work and only where it sets the next step, never as the headline of an item or a group.
- **Outcome is the owner's action.** A planned item is `done` when the owner did their part (the call held, the question sent, the review run); a counterparty's pending answer or a provider date still missing is a carry-over or risk, not `partial`. Before marking an item `unknown`, check whether today's logged meetings or messages with the same people already cover it.
- **Corrections replace.** An owner correction changes the record to the corrected fact; it does not add a note that the earlier reading was wrong or why ("was expected, not unfinished").
- **One fact once.** In a draft or planning file each fact (a carry-over, a date, a risk, a boundary) appears in one section; other sections name it in a few words or omit it. Yesterday's results stay in yesterday's file and are linked, not retold.
- **No empty sections.** Leave out a section or line with nothing to report: no candidates, no captures, nothing archived, checks passed. Optional sections such as what was learned appear only with the owner's own insight or a specific decision or lesson that changes later work; a generic summary of the day is not one.
- **Stalled carry-over.** A carry-over moved twice without progress gets one plain question in the next draft: do it on a named day, wait for a named fact, or drop it. Never write it as "choose the next step of …"; an item waiting for a fact is recorded as waiting for that fact.
- **Closing lines.** Status only when something is blocked or incomplete; Git only state that needs the owner's action under the policy; never announce internal steps, files created as empty companions (a paired WeekReport) or what is left untouched ("existing files are not changed", "the morning plan stays as history", backups, checks run).
- **Plain language.** Speak to the owner in plain human language. Field names in skill drafts (Praise, Deferred, Not today, Not forgotten, What was learned) describe content; render them as natural headings in the owner's language or drop the heading, never as literal translations. Name plan items and results by what is done or decided, not by a category label ("Maintenance") or a shortened plan phrase; policy titles and method terms are not chat vocabulary. The closing question is an ordinary sentence. Internal method terms (ISO week, baseline, lifecycle, gate, checkpoint, status codes such as `pending`) stay in files, not in chat. End with an offer of the next step, not a procedure instruction.

## Rhythm configuration guard for Day/Week rituals

For Day Open/Close, determine the local date and run `ruby <physical-skill-dir>/../../scripts/check-rhythm.rb day <workspace-root>/memory/day-rhythm-config.yaml` once before locating any planning files. For Week Open/Close use `week` instead of `day`. Resolve the workspace root under Personal policy resolution and the script from the selected skill's physical source, not the session cwd; do not substitute a script from another checkout. The read-only script checks safe YAML parsing, mapping types, and the consumed weekday keys (absent keys alone use the documented defaults), and reports the local date and locale-independent weekday. A nonzero result, missing Ruby, or missing script blocks the ritual: report the reason, do not retry with an improvised parser, and do not discover or mutate planning files. This helper is not a ritual runner and does not grant other script or planning-write permissions. Run its adjacent `test-check-rhythm.rb` only when verifying source changes, not during a ritual.

## Local Git evidence for Day/Week rituals

When the owner's policy selects `gitrepos`, Day/Week Open and Close use it only during the requested ritual, as local read-only evidence. Resolve the configured tool and repository list from that policy; do not discover or rewrite the list. Run `<policy-selected-gitrepos> -c <policy-selected-repos.toml> --since 'YYYY-MM-DD 00:00' --until 'YYYY-MM-DD 00:00' --max-commits 2 --format summary-json` when the selected tool supports that documented read-only mode; otherwise use `--format json` and summarize locally. Substitute dates from the local ritual boundary: yesterday for Day Open, today for Day Close, the previous period for Week Open, and the closing period for Week Close. Use the next day's midnight as `--until` for a complete period; omit it for a period still in progress. Do not rely on relative Git dates such as `monday 00:00`. `--max-commits` limits displayed commits per repository, not the count; summarize the structured output locally rather than loading all rows or subjects into context. In `summary-json`, use `scanned_count` to confirm coverage, inspect every `issues` row (including `status_error` and `log_error`), and use `activity` and `dirty` for bounded results; a dirty row gives counts, not filenames. Only if relevant filenames or further detail are needed, query *exact affected repository paths* with positional `gitrepos --format json`, without `-c`, rather than rescanning the configured list. For a tool without `summary-json`, parse the first scan's JSON array locally: each row has `path`, `commit_count`, `commits`, `working_tree` (`staged`, `modified`, `untracked`), `changes`, and `sync`. Check every unfiltered row and the command exit status: `sync.state: error` or `fetch-failed`, `changes` containing `error` or `not-repository`, and any `log_error` mean that evidence is incomplete. Inspect these before filtering routine clean rows and irrelevant metadata from the summary; do not run a second scan merely to discover JSON shape or to reformat its output. A missing or unfamiliar field is unknown, not clean. In full JSON `working_tree` already carries filenames; do not repeat the full scan just to add `--files`. Never pass `--fetch`, `--pull`, or `--switch-main`; locally cached upstream sync is not proof of current remote state. A failed or incomplete scan is unknown, not clean. Commits come from all refs, while dirty state is current, not dated: neither proves a WP outcome or a push. Apply the policy's noise rule to the report, and reconcile results against WP evidence and owner acceptance. This read-only evidence command is not a ritual, synchronization, or background script; it creates no planning-write or Git-mutation permission.

## Local Git evidence for Session rituals

When the owner's policy selects `gitrepos`, Session Open checks dirty state only for repository paths unambiguously identified by the selected WP/context; Session Close checks only repositories actually touched by the session. Pass exact paths as positional arguments to `<policy-selected-gitrepos> --format json`, without `-c` (paths and config cannot be combined); add `--since 'YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM' --max-commits 2` at Close only if the session start boundary is known. Otherwise report commit/push facts only from established evidence, not an invented time window. Summarize the structured output locally, inspect errors and relevant dirty paths, and do not treat an uninspected repository as clean. Do not scan all configured repositories, infer a WP repository from a name, or run this for a read-only/ad hoc opening with no identified repository. Apply the Day/Week rule's read-only flag, evidence and reporting limits; this check never changes Git or planning state.

## Weekly reconciliation cadence

Personal policy may explicitly select **weekly-only** reconciliation. Otherwise retain the existing per-ritual weekly discovery/projection behavior. Resolve the cadence before discovering weekly files; a missing or conflicting personal policy still blocks the affected action. This option changes when weekly evidence is read and projections are written, not who may approve work or mutations.

Under weekly-only, Session Open/Close and Day Close use WP contexts and registry state without locating, reading, pairing, or writing WeekPlan/WeekReport. Day Open alone reads the current WeekPlan covering today as intent context for selecting focus and constraints, not as an execution gate; check its week identifier, week-year and period, and the paired WeekReport's reciprocal filename and period metadata when the report is present, without reading its factual sections or reconciling anything. A missing or ambiguous plan or mismatched pair is reported as limited guidance or drift, not silently replaced with guessed weekly intent; DayPlan may still proceed from sufficient WP/context and owner evidence. No ordinary Session/Day ritual writes weekly files under weekly-only. Weekly registration/factual projections are non-required, not missing or unfinished work. Preserve approved results and next actions in the WP context for the next explicit weekly review; do not create another reconciliation queue. Day rituals use their normal DayPlan path on every weekday, including configured strategy days. Keep rhythm-config validation and all other applicable guards; skip only its weekly-file substitution. An explicit weekly inspection/amendment request may bring additional named weekly scope back in through its existing gate.

Week Open/Close retain weekly pair validation, truthful intent/fact separation, and their approval gates. Week Close reconstructs facts from WP evidence, including material work outside the old WeekPlan. Weekly selection is not execution authorization: a current owner-approved WP can start without baseline membership, while registration alone never changes weekly commitments. Starting, deferring, completion acceptance, and mutation scope remain explicit decisions. Policy may select the start-versus-defer convention, but cannot turn deferred registration into implementation permission.

Under full reconciliation, the weekly steps of the Session and Day rituals are in [WEEKLY.md](WEEKLY.md).

## Work-product status vocabulary

The work-product statuses are exactly `pending`, `in_progress`, `done`, `blocked`, `merged`, `cancelled`, and `archived`. Write them in `snake_case` in context front matter and registry rows; `in-progress` and other spellings are the same status written wrongly, not a variant. Prose in this adapter may hyphenate when reading as English, but a written value is always `in_progress`. Do not invent a status outside this list; an unlisted or ambiguous value is a stop for owner resolution, never a silent normalization.

Meanings, and what each permits:

- `pending` — registered, not started. Appearing in a plan does not by itself make a WP `in_progress`.
- `in_progress` — actively being worked.
- `blocked` — started but cannot proceed pending an external fact or owner decision. Record what unblocks it. `blocked` is a work state, not an outcome; it never counts as accepted and never authorizes completion archival.
- `done` — the owner accepted an observable result. Only an accepted `done` counts toward accepted-done counts and only it permits completion archival.
- `merged` — scope absorbed by another WP. Name the absorbing WP. Not an accepted result of its own.
- `cancelled` — scope withdrawn without a result. Excluded from accepted-done counts; does not permit completion archival. History and reason stay in the single WP context.
- `archived` — a storage fact, not an outcome. Completion and archival are separate: never replace `done` with `archived` because a context moved.

The owner's registry legend in `DS-strategy/docs/WP-REGISTRY.md` owns the display symbols and remains authoritative for that mapping; this list owns the written values the skills read and write. A registry symbol and a context status that disagree are a stop, not a value to be repaired silently.

`blocked` is also used in these instructions as a ritual outcome — a ritual reports itself blocked when evidence is missing — and as an umbrella-phase state. Those uses describe the ritual or a phase, not the work product's status field; do not copy a ritual outcome into a WP status.

## Work-product context resolution and completion archival

Resolve a work-product context from the governance registry and filesystem rather than assuming one personal layout. First require exactly one row for the stable numeric WP identifier in `DS-strategy/docs/WP-REGISTRY.md`. Then inspect only these supported card shapes for that identifier, treating zero-padded and unpadded numeric forms as the same identifier:

- `DS-strategy/inbox/WP-N-*.md`;
- `DS-strategy/inbox/WP-N/WP-N.md`;
- `DS-strategy/archive/wp-contexts/WP-N-*.md`;
- `DS-strategy/archive/wp-contexts/WP-N/WP-N.md`.

Require exactly one canonical context across both locations. No match, multiple flat files, flat-plus-nested duplicates, inbox-plus-archive duplicates, a mismatched nested card name, or a registry collision is a stop for owner resolution. Do not choose by recency, glob order, shortest path, current status, or preferred layout. Readers use the unique resolved path whether the context is active or archived. Reusable instructions must not impose a flat-to-nested or nested-to-flat migration.

Completion and archival are separate facts. `status: done` records an accepted result; archive location records lifecycle storage. Do not replace `done` with `archived` merely because the context moves. A completion archival may occur only after the owner accepts the observable result and reviews an exact archival plan containing the source, destination, required live-reference updates, expected prior states, and completion checks. Approval to close, report, or register a WP without that exact plan is not archival approval.

The archival transaction (approved archival steps, retry, missed candidates) is in [ARCHIVAL.md](ARCHIVAL.md); read it only before proposing or performing an archival.

**Weekly archival option.** Personal policy may select weekly archival. Then Session Close and Day Close record accepted completion but neither identify, propose nor perform any archival, and Day Close keeps its DayPlan in `current/`. Week Close proposes completed WPs still in the inbox and DayPlans dated within its closing period through the same exact-plan approval and transaction. Day Open and Week Open may still propose cleanup of DayPlans outside an unambiguous current weekly period. The policy may also select monthly weekly-file archival: closed WeekPlans and WeekReports stay in `current/` until Month Close archives the month's pairs, and Week Open neither proposes nor performs that move. Without this option the per-ritual behavior in [ARCHIVAL.md](ARCHIVAL.md) applies.

## Project ledgers as work carriers

Personal policy may declare project ledgers: folders kept by a project method such as the Three Clocks Ledger, with their own capability, decision and communication logs. Work inside a declared ledger is recorded there, not in a WP context:

- Session Open for that project takes its context from the ledger (README change protocol when needed, the capabilities Constraint and Now, the entries the request names); it neither proposes nor registers a WP. Session Close records the result through the ledger's change protocol.
- Day and Week Close read the ledger entries dated in the period (decisions accepted, capabilities built, communications) as evidence, cite their IDs in the results and never propose a WP, retrospective or not, for work the ledger records.
- Load is counted by projects that received attention, not by registry rows.

The WP naming rule does not apply to ledger IDs; show them with their titles where the owner needs them.

## Planning-projection boundaries

The interactive lifecycle owner writes only the projections permitted by the requested ritual and its approval gate. An approved implementation edit to a governance-owned document is not permission for planning reconciliation.

| Durable planning surface | Permitted writer and condition |
|---|---|
| matching WP context | Session Open creates the approved registration or records an explicitly approved activation; Session Close records the result and next action. Day/Week rituals may reconcile only owner-approved factual status, disposition, or next-action changes, without manufacturing a Session Close. |
| WP registry | lifecycle owner writes only an approved registration or status transition; keep execution detail in the WP context. The name column carries only the artifact name, at most 80 characters: no dates, session references, metrics, or commit hashes. |
| `memory/MEMORY.md` | lifecycle owner writes only a global context, blocker, or next-focus change, including an approved new-WP pointer when needed. Edit only the affected line, never rewrite the whole file. It is a current pointer, not a log: replace the line a change supersedes; day and week results stay in DayPlan/WeekReport, with at most one link to the latest; drop lines about closed days and weeks. |
| DayPlan | Day Open or Day Close writes it after its approval gate; Session Open/Close does not insert or mirror work into it. |
| WeekPlan | Week Open owns weekly intent: creation, activation, the frozen commitment baseline, future candidates, and approved amendments. Week Close may change only lifecycle metadata. The bounded exceptions in [WEEKLY.md](WEEKLY.md) permit registration and explicit intent amendments without running another ritual. |
| WeekReport | Week Close owns weekly facts, classifications, unresolved-work dispositions, and the next-week decisions. Session Close and Day Open/Close may add approved factual reconciliation under the bounded exception in [WEEKLY.md](WEEKLY.md). |

The bounded WeekPlan exceptions (registration, factual reconciliation, weekly intent amendment, strategy day) are in [WEEKLY.md](WEEKLY.md); read it whenever weekly files are in scope or an exception is requested.

At every boundary, report uncertain or conflicting evidence rather than normalizing it silently. A `done` transition requires an observable acceptance result and owner acceptance. Captures and exact archival plans may be approved as separately labeled items in the consolidated proposal; Git operations, copied-file deployment, and external operations retain their separate approval gates.
